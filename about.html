About Tidewell

Tidewell is an independent, non-profit institute that measures the coast and shows its working.

Why we exist

Coastal planning decisions, from sea-wall heights to shellfish licences, depend on measurements that few people can inspect. Tidewell was founded in 1995 by a group of oceanographers and harbour engineers who wanted a record that a council officer, a fisher and a graduate student could all read in the same form.

We run the instruments, process the data, and publish both the results and the raw series. When we correct an error, the correction is dated and kept in the changelog of the dataset it affects.

What we do not do

We do not take commissions that restrict publication. We do not forecast individual storms, which is the job of the national weather service. We do not make planning recommendations; we describe what has been measured and how certain we are.

Principles

How we work

Publish the uncertainty

Every figure carries an interval and a note on how it was derived. A number without an error bar is a draft.

Keep the raw data

Processed series are always accompanied by the unprocessed readings, so a different correction can be tried.

Prefer simple instruments

A sensor that can be repaired on a boat in the rain beats one that is marginally more precise but needs a laboratory.

Write for the non-specialist first

Each paper has a plain-language summary, and each dataset has a one-page guide written before the data is released.

Funding and governance

About 58% of our income comes from multi-year grants from public research councils, 27% from regional harbour authorities that fund station maintenance without editorial rights, and 15% from training courses. The full breakdown is published each March.

An independent board of seven oversees the budget. A separate scientific advisory panel reviews methods annually and publishes its report unedited.
